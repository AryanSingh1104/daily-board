# Daily trivia board: category research and proposed rotation

Research date: October 2, 2026.

**Historical design exploration:** The beta now uses fill-in blanks, matching, and set naming. Each column has 2 / 3 / 4 items with 1 / 2 / 3 correct required and one miss allowed. Earlier multiple-choice and unlimited-retry proposals below have been superseded; see the README and category-introduction research for current behavior. This is a product/content recommendation for an original daily game inspired by Daily Orbs and Jeopardy, based on a small public sample, not a comprehensive audit of either game’s question bank.

## Recommendation

Use **eight familiar category families**, but give each daily board column a **specific, fresh theme**. Put three values inside every column: 100, 200, and 300. A player picks any available tile. Family names help players recognize their strengths; narrow themes make tomorrow feel different. Difficulty should increase within a column, not across the four category families.

Example column heading: small label **MUSIC**, large title **Name the Band**, then 100 / 200 / 300 tiles. This keeps the board readable without requiring every day to be generic Music / Movies / Countries / General Knowledge.

## What the reference games actually offer

| Reference | Observed public examples, summarized | Useful lesson for our game |
|---|---|---|
| Daily Orbs | The indexed September 18 board matches country nicknames to countries, television episode titles to series, specialist vocabulary to activities, and Latin phrases to English meanings. The indexed September 29 homepage matches distinctive letters to languages, flag fragments to countries, architectural details to photographs, and lyrics to performers. | A narrow, repeatable premise can produce many editions. The content is more distinctive than simply asking unrelated general-knowledge questions under a broad label. |
| Jeopardy official material | Its writers describe balancing academic, popular-culture, wordplay, and lifestyle material. Evidence: [Daily Orbs September 18](https://dailyorbs.com/2026-09-18), [Daily Orbs homepage, indexed September 29 board](https://dailyorbs.com/), [Jeopardy writers’ process](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/cast-crew/inside-jeopardy-writers-room), [Jeopardy published category examples](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/clues-categories/can-you-run-these-nine-categories-ryan-long).

The public Daily Orbs sample supports **recurring theme series**, including edition numbers. It does **not** establish that Daily Orbs has a fixed eight-family taxonomy, nor does it reveal its internal scheduling or difficulty algorithm. The eight-family system below is our proposal.

## Eight families, with reusable theme ideas

These are original editorial suggestions, not copied category sets.

| Family | Five rotating themes | Good question material |
|---|---|---|
| **Music** | Name the Band; Album Match; Instrument Case; Soundtrack Connections; Decade Drop | Identify performers from independently written clues about albums, groups, instruments, or film connections. Mix eras and genres. |
| **Movies & TV** | On Location; Character Roll Call; Behind the Camera; From Page to Screen; Animated Worlds | Film settings, characters, directors, adaptations, and animation across several studios. |
| **World & Geography** | Capital Connections; Border Check; Island Hopping; Landmark Locator; Rivers & Ranges | Countries, capitals, borders, islands, monuments, rivers, and mountain ranges. |
| **Science & Nature** | Solar System; Animal Abilities; Everyday Science; Element ID; Plant Life | Stable facts from space, animals, household physics, chemistry, and botany. |
| **Words & Language** | Word Twins; Borrowed Words; Letter Swap; Phrase Finder; Hidden Connection | Double meanings, word origins, one-letter transformations, idioms, and common links. Avoid specialist vocabulary on the easy row. |
| **Food & Everyday Life** | On the Menu; Ingredient Detective; Kitchen Kit; Things We Use; Hobbies at Home | Dishes, ingredients, tools, inventions in daily use, and recognizable hobby terminology. |
| **History & Culture** | Ancient Worlds; Firsts & Inventions; Art Clues; Turning Points; Myth & Legend | Civilizations, historical inventions, visual arts, dated events, and mythology. |
| **Sports & Games** | Name the Sport; Rules of Play; Olympic Events; Tabletop Shelf; Video Game Worlds | Rules, equipment, sporting events, board games, and game settings. Prefer stable rules and history to changing league rosters. |

### Where the subjects overlap

- **Music × Movies:** composers, musical films, or the film associated with a song title. This lets players use more than one area of knowledge.
- **Movies × Geography:** story settings and actual filming locations, explicitly distinguished in the wording.
- **World × Food:** a cuisine, ingredient, or regional dish can point toward a place; avoid disputed single-origin claims.
- **Words × World:** place-name meanings, language associations, or geographic terms.
- **History × Science:** inventions and discoveries, with dates and credit carefully pinned down.

Jeopardy’s official music-film-location feature and its pop-culture writing article provide examples of combining domains. The latter also discusses the danger of making a category’s premise too obscure. Our implication: use playful themes with an immediately understandable one-line instruction. [Music and movie locations](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/clues-categories/hills-are-alive-jeopardy), [Pop-culture writing process](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/clues-categories/inside-pop-culture-jeopardy-writers-room).

## A four-column daily mixing rule

Initial editorial rule, to test with players:

1. **One World & Geography column every day.** Countries are a core part of the requested concept.
2. **One entertainment column:** balance Music and Movies & TV across the week, counting wildcard appearances too.
3. **One knowledge/life column:** rotate Science & Nature, History & Culture, and Food & Everyday Life.
4. **One wildcard column:** Words & Language, Sports & Games, or the entertainment family not already used.

Use four different families per board. No repeated narrow theme inside a 14-day window, and no repeated answer inside a seven-day window. These are proposed editorial safeguards, not researched optimal intervals. Allow several regions and eras in each week. Do not make every entertainment clue depend on recent English-language US releases.

### Example seven-day rotation

| Board | World | Entertainment | Knowledge / life | Wildcard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Capital Connections | Music: Name the Band | Science: Solar System | Words: Word Twins |
| 2 | Landmark Locator | Screen: On Location | Food: Ingredient Detective | Sports: Name the Sport |
| 3 | Border Check | Screen: Character Roll Call | History: Ancient Worlds | Games: Tabletop Shelf |
| 4 | Island Hopping | Screen: From Page to Screen | Science: Animal Abilities | Words: Letter Swap |
| 5 | Rivers & Ranges | Music: Instrument Case | Food: Kitchen Kit | Sports: Olympic Events |
| 6 | Cities by the Sea | Screen: Behind the Camera | History: Firsts & Inventions | Music: Album Match |
| 7 | A Country in Three Clues | Music: Decade Drop | Science: Element ID | Words: Borrowed Words |

This gives Geography seven appearances, Music four, Screen four, Science three, Words three, Sports/Games three, Food two, and History two. That is intentionally weighted toward the requested music/movies/countries mix, while leaving room for discovery.

## Difficulty should come from the clue, not obscurity alone

| Value | Writing target | Example structure |
|---|---|---|
| **100** | A familiar subject, one direct clue, clearly distinct answer choices | Identify a country from a famous capital. |
| **200** | A less obvious association or two linked facts | Identify a country from a neighboring country plus a landmark. |
| **300** | A specific fact or a short deduction, still solvable from fair clues | Identify a country from a uniquely constraining geographic description. |

Use the same answer format across rows initially. Four-choice questions make a quick mobile prototype practical; changing to typed answers at 300 would confound question difficulty with input difficulty. Make the distractors the same type of thing as the answer, plausible, and mutually exclusive. Avoid three obviously wrong options.

Target difficulty bands can be evaluated after playtests: roughly 75–90% correct for 100, 50–70% for 200, and 25–45% for 300 among the intended audience. These are experimental targets, not validated results. A small playtest should identify obvious mismatches before launch.

Each row must be independently answerable: a revealed 100 answer should not accidentally give away 200. Harder points should reward more knowledge or reasoning rather than tricky wording. Jeopardy’s writers describe discussing clarity, multiple possible correct responses, and difficulty at roundtable reviews; a small game can adopt that editorial discipline. [Official writing process](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/cast-crew/inside-jeopardy-writers-room).

## What to put in the website’s category section

Use a compact **“In the rotation”** section below the daily board or on a separate Categories view:

- Eight small cards with family name and a one-line subject description.
- Up to three example themes on expansion.
- A marker on today’s four families, with a “Play today’s board” action.
- A clear sentence: “Four categories arrive each day. Every category has three questions worth 100, 200, and 300.”

Avoid making category browsing change the shared daily board. A future practice mode could let players choose topics; it should be visibly separate from the daily result.

## Original content workflow

For every published question, retain family, theme, value, original prompt, choices, correct answer, short explanation, verification source URL, verification date, writer/reviewer status, and intended publication date. Write new clues from independently verified facts; do not import the reference games’ wording or question banks. Start with text and original simple illustrations; media such as recordings, lyrics, movie stills, logos, and photographs needs its own sourcing and permissions review if introduced. No claim of legal clearance is made here.

Draft three coherent clues for a theme together, then review them without the answer key. Check that one answer fits, the theme is understandable, the three values feel progressively harder, and the subject matter is varied. A 7-day prototype needs 84 questions; a 30-day launch buffer needs 360.

## Access limits and uncertainties

- Daily Orbs’ live pages returned little or no text when directly opened through the web tool. Its public search index exposed the September 18 dated page and the September 29 homepage board. These observations are a small dated sample and should not be presented as its complete catalog or today’s exact board.
- Official Jeopardy pages exposed actual clue examples and writing-process commentary. This is enough to study structure, but it is not a representative statistical sample of all categories.
- A repeated theme family is evidence of reusable editorial patterns, not proof of an automated rotation algorithm.
- The proposed category weights, repetition windows, and difficulty targets need player testing. The prototype’s name, visual identity, and public-launch clearance remain separate decisions.

## Matching prototype follow-up

The user emphasized Daily Orbs' matching mechanic after the initial category research. Keep **subject**, **theme**, **format**, and **difficulty** as separate editorial fields. For example: Countries → Place to Place → Match pairs → 100 / 200 / 300. A format stays consistent down a column; future boards can pair that format with other subjects.

Board 02 now tests a Countries matching column alongside three multiple-choice columns. It contains 2 / 3 / 4 landmark–country pairs. Correct matches stay in place, partial progress is saved, incorrect matches increment a mistake count, and completing a set awards its tile's value once. Unlimited retries deliberately make this an interaction test, not a balanced competitive scoring system. Future tests should compare one free mistake, limited retries, or a clean-completion bonus; none is chosen yet.

Matching themes that fit the same system:

| Subject | Matching premise |
|---|---|
| Music | Artist ↔ album; instrument ↔ instrument family |
| Movies & TV | Character ↔ film; director ↔ film |
| World & Geography | Landmark ↔ country; capital ↔ country |
| Science & Nature | Animal ↔ adaptation; element ↔ symbol |
| Words & Language | Word ↔ definition; borrowed word ↔ source language |
| Food & Everyday Life | Dish ↔ main ingredient; tool ↔ use |

Matching gains enjoyment from elimination, so the last pair being obvious is an intentional payoff. A two-pair set offers only one independent deduction and should be treated as a warm-up. Pair count affects time and elimination as well as difficulty; test obscurity and set size separately before launch. Fill-in-the-blank is a future format, not implemented in this prototype.
